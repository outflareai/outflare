# Sales reporting

Sales reporting sits beside Sales conversations in CRM, with a Reporting link in the Sales manager navigation. Open `/<workspace>/<brand>/sales-reporting`. The default page reads real API data; **View UI demo** opens a separate, explicitly fictional prototype, including when the API is unavailable. A failed request never falls back to examples.

Without a Lead Portal connection, it shows **Outflare data only**: native SDR pickups and confirmed customers remain available, while discovery, research, verification, email, replies, costs and capacity stay unavailable. Confirmed customers do not require payment or a revenue amount; optional expected monthly revenue stays separate from payment reporting.

## Connections and access

`GET /v1/brands/{brand}/sales/reporting` and `/sales/reporting/leads` require `customers.read` and either a signed-in Sales manager or `members.manage`. Automation tokens cannot borrow their creator's Sales manager role. API and MCP share these checks.

Connect **Lead Portal** under Connectors, using that portal's URL and admin `lp_` API key, and assign the connection to its one brand. Credentials stay in the workspace's sealed connector store. A shared connection is deliberately ineligible: a portal report describes the entire source portal, so it cannot safely act like independently filtered data for several brands. Several eligible connections require an explicit choice. No operator or environment key is used.

The source portal must expose `/api/v1/reports/sales-reporting` version 1. Its admin gate applies to session and API-key access. Reporting is read-only apart from the normal API-key last-used timestamp. Balance and mailbox observations are read from saved snapshots; opening a report does not buy tools, refresh provider tokens, enroll leads or send email.

## Counting

- Discovery is the first Sales research queue entry per company domain, or a directly submitted Sales lead's creation if no research job exists.
- Research is the first completed research job timestamp available for that domain. The job store uses `updated_at`, not an immutable completion log. Historical edits to a completed job can change its recorded timestamp.
- Verification is the first `verify → ready_for_outreach` event. Bison enrollment is not another funnel stage. First email uses the earliest confirmed email enrollment send timestamp.
- Reply is the first recorded email reply per company, using the portal's event receipt time. LinkedIn replies and repeat replies do not inflate it. This report deliberately differs from older reports that count every event or reply.
- SDR pickup is the first native Outflare `lead_taken` event with actor role `sdr`. A direct AE pickup does not become an SDR pickup.
- Closed is the first native `customer_confirmed` event. An archive is not evidence of a lost deal. Displayed deal values are the current recorded values, grouped by currency; missing values and unconnected payments are not zero. Historical portal SDR/deal outcomes are not imported by this report.

All ranges are `[from,to)` in UTC. 24/48 hours use complete hours; week/month/year use 7/30/365 complete days. The lines compare independently dated milestones. The **Same leads** cohort follows companies discovered within that period through their latest progress, even if later stages occurred outside the period. Pending or unobserved later milestones are not counted as lost.

The interval inspector requests every selected stage and returns 50 records per stage with independent counts and pagination. The same report lifecycle supplies the counts and lists. Native records link to their canonical Sales conversation; no copied identities are stored in a reporting table.

## Original uploader and agent statistics

Queues, lead details and milestone lists show the original uploader separately from the current Sales owner. Identity is the named discovery worker or API key, never a credential value, hash or prefix. Revoking a key does not erase its historical attribution. Two keys owned by the same user remain distinct; current researchers and Sales owners do not inherit discovery credit.

The portal derives origin from the first accepted discovery event for the company domain. Direct submissions use the first submit event's API-key principal and its owner, a legacy named Sales agent, or an explicitly recorded manual principal. Missing evidence remains Unknown. The report never guesses an uploader from the current research job assignment. Earlier discovery events without structured domain evidence are also Unknown.

Outflare joins authorized native lead source IDs to `GET /api/v1/reports/sales-reporting?lead_ids=...` in batches of at most 100, with at most four concurrent reads and an eight-second overall deadline. Only a brand-owned portal connection can resolve identities. A source outage leaves queues usable and labels origin unavailable. Existing imports work without a backfill, a new engine table, or switching on the outbound bridge. Several portal connections make queue origin unavailable until there is one unambiguous source; reporting can use the explicitly selected connection.

**Agent performance** in Results includes each stage's event-dated activity, with native SDR pickups and closures joined to the same uploader. Counts reconcile with the main totals, including Unknown. Coverage states how many native milestones could be attributed. These are activity counts, not same-cohort conversion rates. The alternate **Submission quality** view counts submitted, accepted, duplicate, blocked and held discovery candidates, deduplicated per worker/job/domain using the latest outcome in the selected window. Direct submission keys have no equivalent intake ledger and show unavailable quality, not zero. Cost is not allocated per agent without supporting usage evidence.

## Costs and capacity

Cost windows end at the most recent complete UTC hour, matching the source's hourly spend ledger. Each tool and the combined Sales total divide by verified companies in those same 24 hours. AI charges already booked by workers are not added again. Shared metered usage is shown separately with **Unallocated** per-lead cost, never charged in full to Sales. Wallet balances may serve several tools or teams and are not summed. Missing coverage, unavailable prices/balances and zero denominators remain explicit. Recorded tool usage excludes mailbox subscriptions and staff time.

Mailbox capacity uses the source report's Sales department mapping. Configured slots are physical capacity, not a reserved Sales quota; shared campaigns, stale snapshots, unknown limits, source freshness and provider day/timezone remain visible.

## Discovery

A connected service may add an optional `discovery` object to its version 1 summary, describing a system that finds companies before research: `missions` (name, state, total spend and spending cap in cents), `totals` and `buckets` counting companies `found`, `new` (not already held), `passed` (screening), `graded_ab` and `sent` (to research) in the requested range, `lanes` (total, a count per state, and up to 50 lanes with all-time found, new and passed) and `spend` (cents in the range and per tool). It holds counts and cents only, never a company or a contact. The connector checks it separately from the rest of the report: an unknown count, a negative or non-numeric value or an oversized list drops the block to `null` and the stages, costs and capacity still show. The **Discovery** view shows it; a tool with no recorded cost shows — and is never counted as zero. A lane's pass rate is passed over new.

### Discovery lanes from Outflare's own lead finder

On a server that runs the lead finder (Outflare Cloud, capability `lead_finder`), the Discovery view starts with the **Lane leaderboard** of a profile (a picker when the brand has more than one). It reads `GET /v1/brands/{brand}/cloud/lead-finder/profiles/{id}/lanes/board` every 30 seconds and shows the yield (found, new, screened, passed, cost per passed company, requests, lanes by status), the lanes filtered by state (Scaling, Probing, Proposed, Dead, Exhausted) with found, new %, passed of screened, estimated size, cost per pass and what each taught, the recipe on expand, **By way of finding** (per kind of lane and signal) and the **Shared record** (the notes, and the exact text an agent reads). A lane opens as its own page (not a drawer): its recipe, why it has its status, its lesson, its counters with the reasons companies were known or repeats, stopped at intake and graded, its notes, its companies (`GET .../lanes/companies?lane_id=`) with stage, grade and stop reason, and a person's controls: approve, reject, pause, resume, priority, revisit date and edit recipe (`PATCH .../lanes/{lane}`; reject and pause ask why). **Add a lane** is a page too (`POST .../lanes`). Statuses are set by the server's rules, never by this page; lane text is agents' and shown as text. A server without the lane registry answers 404 and the leaderboard is left out. Every click is a `ui.sales_reporting_interacted` event with a bounded `action` (`finder_board_*`), and every error shown is `ui.error_shown`.

Below it, **Lane flow, live**: the companies discovery agents and the lead finder's own sources send into the brand's flows, by lane, live (it reads again every 30 seconds while the page is open). It needs no Lead Portal connection, and a workspace that still has one sees the portal's sections below it, unchanged. On a server without the lead finder the view is the portal's alone.

It reads `GET /v1/brands/{brand}/cloud/lead-finder/lanes?days=` (the page's period in days: 1, 2, 7, 30 or 365; open runs are always included) and, for a lane's own page, `GET .../lanes/companies?run_id=&source=&lane=&stage=`, the hosted extension's routes, called without the typed client as the Lead finder page does. A lane is one way of looking for companies: the lane a discovery agent names when it proposes companies, or one of the lead finder's own sources. The board shows what each lane sent, how many the workspace already had, its new companies at each stage (being checked, not a fit, waiting for review, being added, finding people, ready for the campaign, in People, in the campaign, stopped), fit, people found and in the campaign, and why companies stopped. Opening a lane shows its own page (not a drawer): its stages, why its companies stopped or wait, and its companies a page at a time, filtered by a stage, each with the agent that found it and its people; a company or person in People opens their record.

The counting rules: a company counts once per flow, in the lane that sent it first; one the workspace had counts only as already known; every company is at exactly one stage, so a lane's stages add up to its companies and its list at a stage is exactly the companies the count shows. "In the campaign" is a person enrolled in the profile's campaign (pushed to the connected sequencer when the campaign is linked to one). These numbers are not part of the funnel above (discovered, researched, verified, emailed), which still comes from the connected portal. How a discovery agent sends companies is in [docs/discovery-agents.md](../discovery-agents.md).

### Lanes

`GET /v1/brands/{brand}/sales/reporting/lanes` (optional `status`, `offset`, `integration`) reads the connected service's lane board with `lanes=1` (and `lane_status`, `offset`) on the same reporting endpoint. A lane is one way of searching for companies. The service answers `{"lanes": {"total", "offset", "rows"}}` with up to 50 rows, the lanes that passed the most companies first; each row has `id`, `title`, `status`, optional `method`, `origin`, `segment`, `idea`, `why`, `verdict`, `learned`, a `recipe` of up to five lists (`queries`, `urls`, `seeds`, `describe`, `jobs`, at most 12 strings each) and whole counts `found`, `new`, `known`, `screened`, `passed`. The connector refuses a page with an unknown recipe list, a non-text item, a negative count or a missing title. There is no native part: without a connection `lanes` is null. In the Discovery view, **Search lanes** filters by state, pages 50 at a time and opens a lane to show its recipe, reasoning and lessons. Lane text is written by the connected service and is shown as text only. The `get_sales_discovery_lanes` MCP tool reads the same pages.

## Results and funding

Results compares the current window with the immediately preceding window of the same length, fetched through the same reporting endpoint and brand connection. It shows first replies, SDR pickups, confirmed customers, their recorded expected monthly revenue and the average per valued customer. This uses the Sales workflow's monthly amount, not a lifetime contract value or collected payment. Each period uses event dates; these ratios are activity growth, not same-lead conversion. Zero baselines have no percentage, missing comparisons stay unavailable, and currencies are never added together. The throughput chart appears only in Live flow.

Funding links use a fixed set of provider destinations keyed by the paying wallet. Email finding, BounceBan and LinkedIn lookups funded through Treg link to Treg; AI links to OpenRouter. Unknown wallets have no guessed link. Opening a provider page does not make a payment. The browser records only the bounded wallet key.

## Set up reporting

1. Open Sales reporting for your brand. Native Outflare SDR pickups and confirmed customers are available without a Lead Portal connection. Use **View UI demo** to explore fictional examples, then **Return to live reporting** to see your brand's data.
2. For upstream milestones, connect a compatible Lead Portal service that exposes the version 1 reporting API described above. Assign the connection exclusively to the brand whose pipeline it represents.
3. Verify the connected status, milestone counts, interval lists, cost windows and mailbox observation timestamps. Unavailable source data remains explicitly marked. Connecting reporting does not import leads, activate campaign dispatch or send email.

Outflare's native conversation records and customer-confirmation events supply its Sales outcomes. The connected service supplies its upstream milestones. Reporting reads those sources; it does not transfer their records or database ownership.

## Validation

Run `pnpm -r build`, `pnpm --filter @outflare/web test:sales-reporting-results` and `pnpm --filter @outflare/web test:sales-reporting`. The browser test starts a local Vite server with fictional API responses, covers manager navigation, the Discovery view (the Lane leaderboard, its state filter, a lane's own page and its controls at phone width, and the live flow by lane), unavailable source data, explicit demo access after errors, all periods, optional customer amounts, mobile layout, selected-stage drilldowns, comparison retries and navigation to native conversations. Set `OUTFLARE_TEST_SHOTS` to a local directory for screenshots of these fixtures only. With `TEST_DATABASE_URL` pointing to a disposable database, run `go test ./engine/internal/api -run 'TestSalesReporting|TestSalesAttribution'` and `go test ./engine/internal/connectors/leadportal`. The result tests cover equal periods, missing history, zero baselines, separate currencies and the fixed funding destinations; API tests cover access and brand isolation as well as interval details.

For a UI walkthrough, use fictional fixtures. Select several stages and inspect an interval; switch through all five periods; verify that Results has growth comparisons without throughput; make the previous-period request fail and retry it; and check that each funding link follows its paying wallet.

### Execution history

The Discovery view reads `GET /sales/reporting/lanes?trace=true`, with optional `mission`, `lane`, `run` UUIDs and an `offset`. The same manager/owner and brand-owned connector checks apply. The connector forwards `trace=1` to the source portal. The response keeps `lanes:null` and adds `trace`: the latest 20 recorded runs, tool totals, lane yield, and 50 immutable receipts per page. Selecting a run or opening a lane narrows that history. Refresh explicitly to see new work. An unfinished run without recent receipts is not presented as proof of a healthy process.

A receipt includes its run, lane and company attribution, phase, worker, provider/tool, timestamp, duration, attempt, units, nullable recorded price, bounded request fields and result evidence. Source credentials and request headers must never enter receipts; URLs omit user information, query parameters and fragments. Source content is shown as text, never interpreted as instructions. The connector validates shapes, bounds and forbidden credential fields. Existing reporting read metrics and alerts include `trace` and `lanes`; API reads and UI inspections use the existing product event path without search text or company identities in analytics.

Costs describe provider receipts, not new charges or a second spend ledger. Unknown prices remain unknown and partial per-lead costs are labelled. Mission-level exploration is not arbitrarily assigned to a lane. Quality follows the current outcomes of distinct companies examined in the selected runs, including carried-over companies; A/B is a grading outcome, not a sale. Old runs without instrumentation have no fabricated history. No provider call is made by opening this view.

## Team activity

Sales → Team activity (also linked from Reporting) is a native report available to workspace administrators and this brand's Sales managers. It works without a Lead Portal connection. SDRs and AEs cannot read other people's reporting through this endpoint.

`GET /v1/brands/{brand}/sales/reporting/team` requires explicit RFC3339 `from` (inclusive) and `to` (exclusive), at most 366 days. The UI defaults to Today using the viewer's local midnight through refresh time, with Yesterday, Last 7 days and Last 30 days options. Calendar boundaries use the browser timezone, including daylight-saving changes.

The summary includes enabled SDRs/AEs with zero activity and historical actors who are no longer enabled. Sections use current roles, or the last recorded Sales role for former members. Counts are distinct conversation IDs per actor and action within the range, not clicks or messages. Assignment is credited to the person performing it, not the recipient; `lead_taken` means the person started the workflow, even if its queued introduction subsequently fails. `handoff_completed` is written only when the provider accepts a handoff and ownership transfers. A queued/failed handoff does not count as completed. `send_accepted` is provider acceptance, not delivery; archive is not a lost deal.

The log retains repeated actions, timestamps and the role recorded when acting. `user_id`, `action` and literal person/company `search` filter only the log. Pagination is 50 rows using `(occurred_at,id)` cursors; use the same range and filters on subsequent requests. Brand and workspace scope are enforced on every query, and deleted/merged records are excluded consistently from both summary and log. No message bodies or encrypted event payloads are exposed by this report.

Seen evidence is appended to `sales_events` when the user opens a conversation or explicitly marks it seen, in the same transaction as `sales_seen` and its audit event. Queue display is not a seen action. Repeated reads count once per conversation in summaries. Prior Seen actions are read from retained `audit_events` rows without a Sales event link, joined through the actor to the user and the scoped lead to its canonical record. Their historical role is labeled unrecorded. New audit entries have an `event_id` and are excluded from that legacy source to avoid duplicate counts/log entries. The mutable `sales_seen` timestamp is never used to reconstruct historical days. Existing assignment, archive, draft, send and other events remain reportable. Completed-transfer event history begins with this feature's installation; absence of older transfer events is not proof that no work happened.
