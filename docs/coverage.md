# Coverage implementation

Coverage stores a brand's external placements, publisher relationships and commercial delivery. Customer-facing usage documentation belongs at [outflare.ai/docs](https://outflare.ai/docs).

## Resources and permissions

The API lives under `/v1/brands/{brand}/coverage`. Publishers reference CRM company records; multiple publisher domains can reference the same company, and each website can have multiple placements. A placement may have no URL yet, and different inventory positions on one URL remain distinct. Format, commercial arrangement, pipeline stage and observed page health are independent fields.

`coverage.read` and `coverage.manage` cover placements, maintenance and discovery. Finance resources require `coverage.finance.read` or `coverage.finance.manage`. Agreement approval additionally requires `coverage.approve` and a human principal. CRM conversation context and recorded attribution retain their existing CRM and analytics permissions. Private saved views remain private to their creator. Every resource and reference is scoped to workspace and brand.

Payments are immutable entries with explicit reversals. Agreement amounts, allocations, invoices, credits and payments use integer currency minor units. Reports separate commitments, invoiced and paid amounts, and never add currencies together. Editing approved terms invalidates approval. Recording or approving an agreement never purchases inventory or sends money.

## Observation and maintenance

`coverage.check` runs polite page reads with safe URL handling, robots and opt-out checks. The connected reader uses the workspace's explicit crawl connection and budget. Native reads remain available without a provider. Jobs preserve observed link destinations, anchors and rel tokens.

Page availability is separate from verified brand presence. First verification requires a positive mention or backlink observation. A network failure preserves previous positive evidence. Two agreeing observations are needed for confirmed page loss or missing/changed expected links. A rotating ad or newsletter cannot be declared delivered or absent from one sampled page; use the delivery records and evidence instead.

Fixed maintenance rules create owned work for publication deadlines, confirmed losses, first verification, earned relationship review and potential accuracy problems. Finance reminders have separate permissions. Repeated signals do not duplicate open work. Dismissing an incident suppresses the same evidence; recovery followed by another loss can create a new incident. Snoozing work does not stop monitoring.

## AI assistance

`coverage.review` uses the workspace's model and budget to compare page evidence with published customer-facing Knowledge. Results retain source versions, the external quote and the retrieved Knowledge passage. Source edits invalidate old results; withdrawn Knowledge is removed from returned assessments. Classification and outreach drafts are suggestions for review, with thank-you and link requests as distinct purposes. No scraped instructions receive tools or execute actions.

Placements can opt into fixed scheduled accuracy checks with `monitor_accuracy`. Cadence is at least 24 hours and no more frequent than page monitoring. Unchanged page text, purpose and retrieved Knowledge versions reuse an existing successful assessment before a model call. Paused and archived placements are excluded. Review findings create work; sending outreach remains a separate approved action.

`coverage.extract` reads explicitly supplied commercial document text and returns quoted proposed agreement fields. Values are independently validated and restricted to supported commercial fields. Changing the source version invalidates its extraction. Applying selected suggestions opens the ordinary agreement form; validation and reapproval still apply.

## Plays write placements

Coverage is the shared record of where the brand appears, so the plays that produce an appearance write to it themselves (`engine/internal/coverage`, `coverage.Record`), in their own transaction, instead of each module tracking appearances its own way: a link building prospect whose link is checked live becomes a `resource` (or `partner`) placement with the link as evidence; press coverage a person confirms becomes a `news` placement with the article's date; a review profile found becomes a `review` placement. Each gets its publisher (a company record for the page's domain), a `live` stage, an `up` observation from the play's source and its history, and a discovery receipt so reconciliation never offers the same page as a candidate again. A page that already has a placement is verified again rather than added twice: uniqueness is the page and its position (`url`, `inventory`), never the domain. A link that goes missing is an observation (`coverage.Observe`), and the monitor's rules decide the stage from the observations, as for any placement. A submission or a pitch accepted is never a placement; only the observed appearance is. A partner's task submission a person approves (a creator's post, a partner's page; `engine/internal/api/partner_tasks.go`) is one: its page becomes a `video`, `newsletter`, `podcast`, `community`, `blog` or `partner` placement by where it was published, `affiliate`, `paid` or `earned` by the program, with the approval and the link check as evidence. Sponsorships come from agreements: a deliverable without a placement gets one on the agreement's publisher at the `agreed` stage, due when the deliverable is (`newsletter`, `podcast`, `video` or `blog` by its unit, else `sponsorship`), and a deliverable a person accepts moves it to `live`, dated that day, with the acceptance in its history. A page check can verify a link; it cannot prove a newsletter or a podcast ran, so that placement's health stays with the monitor and the acceptance is the publisher's claim a person took.

## Partnership pipelines

Presence's **Partnerships** tab keeps a board per kind of partnership, each with its own stages and fields: **SEO links** (Prospect, Pitched, Replied, Negotiating, Agreed, Content sent, Live, Declined, No reply), **Sponsorships** (Prospect, Contacted, Media kit received, Negotiating, Booked, Creative sent, Ran, Reported, Declined), **Integrations** (Prospect, Contacted, Scoping, Building, Listing submitted, Live, Paused, Declined), **Affiliates** (Prospect, Contacted, Interested, Terms agreed, Signed up, Active, Inactive, Declined; affiliates only, not resellers) and **Comparison sites** (Prospect, Contacted, Trial account given, Testing, Listed, Ranked, Declined). Each pipeline is a CRM record type that a setup kit adds to the brand's catalog (`playbooks/kits/partnerships-*.yaml`, `POST /v1/brands/{brand}/kits/{id}/apply`, `catalog.manage`), so a partner is a CRM record with its history, linked to its company by its `company` field, and its fields can be changed in the catalog like any other.

**Add prospect** (`POST /v1/brands/{brand}/partnerships`, MCP `add_partnership`, `customers.manage`) takes a pipeline's `type` and the partner's `website`: it finds or adds the company record for the site's domain, then adds the partnership at the first stage, or `stage`, with any other `attributes`. A company already in that pipeline comes back as it is, with `created` false, so a partner is never in one pipeline twice. A pipeline with a **Domain rating** field gets the site's rating from what the workspace's SEO tool already read (its backlinks to the brand and competitor scans), written as enrichment so a value someone types wins; with none read, it stays empty. Cards move between stages on the board like any CRM board, and open as their record.

When a partnership reaches the stage where it shows up somewhere, Presence keeps it (the `partnerships.sync` job, queued by every change to a partnership and by the daily coverage run for changes made in bulk): an SEO partnership at **Live** with **Their page** becomes a placement of that page (a blog or resource placement, paid when it had a price) and an integration at **Live** with its **Listing** an `integration` placement, a comparison site at **Listed** or **Ranked** with its **Comparison page** a `comparison` placement, each checked straight away and then like any placement. A sponsorship at **Booked** records a *proposed* agreement with its publisher for its price and run dates (finance approves it as usual), and at **Ran** a `paid` placement under that agreement, dated the run, with no page to check. Each happens once per partnership, however often its card moves, and is recorded as `partnership.placement_added` or `partnership.agreement_added`. A partner whose page isn't filled in yet gets its placement once it is. Affiliates don't make placements: they follow the partner program instead, and Outflare sends them nothing. Affiliates often join outside the pipeline (a product adds a partner through the API when someone makes their affiliate link), so a new partner whose customer record is an affiliate partnership's **Contact**, or whose work email's domain is its company's, moves that partnership to **Signed up** and fills in the contact; their first referred sale moves it to **Active**; and each sale or refund updates **Referred customers** and **Referred revenue** (the `partnerships.affiliate` job, recorded as `partnership.affiliate_synced`). An affiliate already Active stays; one marked Inactive or Declined comes back when they join.

## Discovery and reports

Reconciliation imports the brand's existing news coverage, backlink and link-prospect sources. Each call processes up to 2,000 unseen source records, records receipts for invalid inputs as well as accepted candidates, and returns `has_more` for continuation. Canonical URLs deduplicate pages while history preserves each source. Provider discovery does not establish publication or verify delivery.

**Found** (`GET /coverage/found`, MCP `list_coverage_found`) lists every find grouped by site. OutflareBot reads each new find once (a bounded, robots-aware page read; backlinks need none) and records its `verdict`: `links` (links to one of the brand's domains), `mentions` (names its website), `name_only` (only its name, which another product can share), `absent` or `unreachable`. The **About you** view shows the unreviewed `links` and `mentions` pages, sites with a link first; **Maybe** shows `name_only`, unread and still-unchecked ones; **Not about you** the dismissed and `absent` ones. `POST /coverage/found/review` (MCP `review_coverage_found`) answers `about_us` or `not_us` for pages by id or every page of a site: `about_us` makes each a live placement under the site's publisher, adding the publisher and its company record when there is none. `spam` marks the whole site as spam: every page on it that isn't a placement moves to **Not about you** tagged spam, and any page found on that site later arrives as spam, so it never reaches the review list. `recheck` hides a page (one whose site is offline, say) for 7 days in **Check again** (`view=later`, with its `recheck_at`); the daily discovery's read then reads it again, and it comes back with what that read found. The app asks these as **Yes, track it**, **No, hide it**, **Spam site** and **Check again in a week**. Presence's follow-ups (the work its maintenance rules create) are the **Follow-ups** view of the same page. The report's `found` counts every page and site found, reviewed or not. Search results that come back as Google redirect links (`google.com/goto?url=...`) are resolved to the page they lead to, or left out when they can't be.

Found is a table, one row per site, with the site's numbers: its domain rating and traffic (from competitor scans), how often AI answers cited it in the last 90 days, how many competitors are on it, which sources found it, and when. It sorts by any of them (`sort`), filters by source, minimum domain rating and text, and can hide the brand's tracked competitors' own sites (`exclude_competitors`). A site whose backlinks to the brand look like spam carries `spam_score` (its worst link's score, 0 to 100, from the SEO tool's own judgement such as Ahrefs' spam flag and Outflare's link checks; [seo.md](seo.md#link-spam)) and `spam_reasons`, shown as **Possible spam** from 30 and **Likely spam** from 60. It is a hint only: nothing is hidden until a person answers **Spam site**. More sites load as you scroll.

**Opportunities** (`GET /coverage/opportunities`, MCP `list_coverage_opportunities`) is the same kind of table: one row per page, or one per site with `group=domain` (its best opportunity, and `site_opportunities` says how many it has; `host` lists one site's). It sorts by leverage or by any number, filters by minimum domain rating, and `exclude_competitors=true` hides competitors' own sites, which rarely link to a rival (on by default in the app). A page's translations (`/fr/...`, `fr.site...`, `?lang=fr`) count as its English page: competitors found on any of them make one opportunity on the English address (`coverage.EnglishURL`), with `evidence.translations` set. Presence has its own item in the app's menu.

Reports distinguish discovery, claimed publication, first verified presence and imported baseline inventory. Page availability/loss history, retention evidence windows and unknown observations have explicit definitions. Commercial delivery reports remain source claims; overlapping submissions can overlap. The attribution endpoint reads actual recorded referrer sessions and attribution credits, deduplicated by publisher website, with model/window and currency labels. It requires analytics permission and does not infer causal lift.

Report snapshots are immutable server-computed results. They exclude live privileged finance and attribution panels. Exports preserve the displayed snapshot instead of silently replacing it with current figures.

## Link value

Where competitors appear (`GET /coverage/competitor_placements`, the competitor page's placements and Presence's **Where competitors appear**) is sorted by what each link is worth to the brand, not by the site's Domain Rating alone (`engine/internal/coverage/value.go`). Each page gets:

- `access`, how the link is got: `cant_buy` (an editorial review, roundup or comparison that only being good gets you into), `earned` (an article, guide or mention someone chose to write), `paid` (looks paid for, with evidence), `free` (a profile, directory, listing, forum post, video or social post anyone can make) or `unknown`.
- `value`, 0 to 100: the page's authority (its own URL rating, and the domain's rating, except on big platforms such as YouTube or Google where anyone can post, so their domain counts for nothing), its readers (domain traffic), trust (followed, not paid, rating backed by real traffic) and relevance (how many of the brand's search topics the page's title and anchor share, from the keywords its own sites rank for, else its competitors'; brand and competitor names don't count; and how many competitors the same page features). The total is weighted by `access`, so a free profile on a DR 100 platform ranks below an earned review on a DR 50 site.
- `value_reasons`, the short reasons behind the score.

Values are worked out when a scan saves pages, and for older rows on the next list read. `access=` filters by how the link is got, `order=` sorts by `value` (default), `domain_rating` or `recent`. Opportunities use the same classification: a free-to-get link scores lower and platforms get no SEO points (`factors.access`).

## Validation

`TestBrandCoverage*` exercises brand isolation, permissions, money constraints, imports, source receipts, page loss/recovery, task recurrence, fixed schedules, workspace-only provider credentials, quoted AI results, version invalidation, snapshots and attribution deduplication. The standard API contract validator checks requests, responses and scope declarations. Run the repository race-test shards and regenerate both SDKs after contract changes.
